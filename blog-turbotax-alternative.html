The TurboTax Problem for Immigrants

TurboTax is the #1 tax filing software in the US. For simple W-2 returns, it works great. But if you're an immigrant, expat, or anyone with financial ties to 2+ countries, TurboTax has three fatal flaws:

  1. Single-country tunnel vision. TurboTax only files US returns. If you owe taxes in Canada, India, Germany, or Finland too, you're on your own.
  2. Tax treaty blindness. The US has tax treaties with 60+ countries that can reduce or eliminate withholding taxes. TurboTax doesn't analyze them.
  3. Foreign reporting landmines. FBAR, FATCA, T1135, Schedule FA — TurboTax will flag these but won't tell you about the equivalent forms in your other country of residence.

And the kicker? Foreign income support requires the Self-Employed tier at $89+. Over 5 years, that's $445+ for software that still can't tell you whether your Finnish residence card affects your US tax residency.

Why Immigrants Need More Than TurboTax

If you have any of these situations, TurboTax alone won't cut it:

  • 🔬 Dual tax residency — You might owe taxes in two countries simultaneously. The 183-day test and Substantial Presence Test determine residency differently.
  • 📜 Tax treaty benefits — The US-India treaty reduces dividend withholding from 30% to 15%. The US-Canada treaty prevents double taxation on employment income. TurboTax doesn't calculate these.
  • ⚠️ Foreign reporting penalties — Miss FBAR ($10K+ penalty), FATCA (up to $60K), Canada's T1135 ($2,500+), or France's Form 3916 (€1,500+) and you'll wish you'd known about them.
  • 🏦 Multi-country bank accounts — Each country has its own reporting threshold. US: $10K aggregate (FBAR). Canada: $100K cost (T1135). India: no reporting, but DTAA claims required.
  • 📊 Income splitting across countries — If you earn salary in Canada and freelance income from US clients, which country taxes what? Treaty articles determine this — TurboTax can't.
