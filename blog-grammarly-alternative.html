The Problem with Grammarly in 2026

Grammarly was built for the pre-AI era. Its core promise — fix your grammar — is increasingly commoditized. But the new writing problem isn't typos. It's AI slop: the generic, soulless, pattern-heavy text that ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini produce by default.

Here's what Grammarly doesn't do well:

  • Detect AI-generated content patterns — Grammarly's tone detector flags "formal" vs "casual" but misses AI slop signals
  • Humanize robotic text — Grammarly's rewrites often make AI text more formulaic, not less
  • Catch 250+ AI-specific patterns — hedging language, vague qualifiers, repetitive structures, cliché transitions
  • Score your text across 8 slop dimensions — Grammarly gives a generic "correctness" score

In 2026, the #1 writing quality issue isn't "your vs you're." It's "does this sound like a robot wrote it?"
