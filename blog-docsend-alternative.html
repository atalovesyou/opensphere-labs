Why Freelancers Are Ditching DocSend

DocSend charges $15/user/month ($180/year) for basic proposal tracking. That's steep when you're a freelancer sending 3-10 proposals a month. Here's what you're actually paying for:

  • Page-by-page analytics (which pages clients read, how long)
  • Open notifications (know when they open your proposal)
  • Link expiration & password protection
  • Version control (update docs after sending)

These are useful features. But $180/year to know if someone read your PDF? There's a better way.

#2: DocSend — The Incumbent (But Pricey)

DocSend (owned by Dropbox) is the market leader. It works well but you're paying for enterprise features you probably don't need:

  • $15/mo for basic analytics
  • $50/mo for team features + Salesforce integration
  • No heatmap view — you get page times but not visual heat
  • No AI-generated follow-up suggestions
  • Requires account creation + subscription

Best for: Teams already in the Dropbox ecosystem who need version control and CRM integrations.

#3: PandaDoc — Enterprise Document Suite

PandaDoc is a full document management platform — proposals, contracts, e-signatures, payments. Overkill if you just want to know if your client read your proposal.

  • Starts at $35/mo per seat
  • Includes e-signatures and payment collection
  • No heatmap or engagement scoring
  • 14-day free trial, then expensive

Best for: Agencies that need proposals + contracts + e-signatures in one platform.

#4: Proposify — Agency-Focused ($49/mo)

Proposify is built for agencies with proposal templates, client management, and analytics. But at $49/mo, it's the most expensive option:

  • Beautiful proposal templates
  • Content library for reusable sections
  • Client management dashboard
  • No engagement heatmap or AI follow-ups
  • 14-day trial, then $49-59/mo

Best for: Design and marketing agencies sending 20+ proposals/month.

#5: Better Proposals — Mid-Tier at $19/mo

Better Proposals splits the difference between DocSend and Proposify:

  • Interactive proposal builder
  • Electronic signatures included
  • Basic open/view tracking
  • 14-day trial, then $19/mo

Best for: Freelancers who want a proposal builder with basic tracking.

#6: Qwilr — Web Proposal Pages ($35/mo)

Qwilr turns proposals into interactive web pages instead of PDFs:

  • Interactive quote blocks, image galleries, videos
  • Analytics on page views
  • Stripe integration for payments
  • No free tier — starts at $35/mo

Best for: Freelancers who prefer web proposals over PDFs.

#7: Bonsai — All-in-One Freelance Suite ($24/mo)

Bonsai (HelloBonsai) bundles proposals, contracts, invoicing, and time tracking:

  • Decent proposal tracking analytics
  • Includes contracts + invoicing (like our InvoicePilot)
  • $17-39/mo depending on plan
  • No heatmap or AI follow-ups

Best for: Freelancers who want an all-in-one tool and don't mind paying for it.

#8: Attach — Budget Option ($9/mo)

Attach is the cheapest paid option with basic document tracking:

  • 5 free documents, then $9/mo
  • Basic open/view analytics
  • Simple and lightweight
  • Limited features compared to others

Best for: Occasional proposal tracking on a tight budget.
