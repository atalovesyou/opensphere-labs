What Is AI Slop?

AI slop is the specific class of low-quality, formulaic writing that large language models produce by default. It's not wrong — it's bland. It's the literary equivalent of elevator music: technically correct, completely forgettable.

"In today's rapidly evolving digital landscape, it's crucial to leverage cutting-edge solutions that drive meaningful engagement. By harnessing the power of innovative technologies, organizations can unlock unprecedented opportunities for growth and transformation."

That paragraph says nothing. Yet thousands of similar paragraphs are published every hour. That's slop.

"Most marketing tools waste your time. Here's the one that actually moved our conversion rate from 2.1% to 3.8% in six weeks."

See the difference? Specific, direct, human. No hedging, no filler, no jargon.

The 8 Dimensions of AI Slop

Not all slop is created equal. UnSlop.ai scores text across 8 distinct dimensions to pinpoint exactly where writing sounds robotic:

1. Hedging Language — "arguably", "it could be said", "one might consider", "it's worth noting". AI models hedge everything because they're trained to never be wrong. Humans have opinions.
2. Filler Phrases — "in today's rapidly evolving landscape", "at the end of the day", "it goes without saying". Zero information content, pure word count padding.
3. Robotic Transitions — "furthermore", "moreover", "additionally", "in addition to the aforementioned". Nobody talks like this. Real writers use "also", "but", or just start the next sentence.
4. Vague Superlatives — "game-changing", "revolutionary", "unprecedented", "cutting-edge", "state-of-the-art". If everything is revolutionary, nothing is.
5. Passive Voice Overuse — "it should be noted that", "it has been demonstrated", "efforts were undertaken". Passive voice is the safe choice. Safe writing is invisible writing.
6. Repetitive Structure — Every paragraph starts with a topic sentence, has three supporting points, and ends with a transition. AI loves the 5-paragraph essay format. Real writing varies rhythm.
7. Unnecessary Qualifiers — "quite", "rather", "somewhat", "relatively", "fairly". These weaken every sentence they touch. "Somewhat important" means "not important enough to say clearly."
8. Corporate Jargon — "leverage", "synergy", "stakeholder", "pivot", "circle back", "value proposition". Business buzzwords that mean nothing specific.

Step 4: Humanize the Flagged Sections

For each flagged pattern:

  • Hedging → Pick a side. "Arguably the best" becomes "the best." Commit.
  • Filler → Delete it. "In today's rapidly evolving landscape" becomes nothing. Start with your point.
  • Robotic transitions → Use "but", "and", "so", or nothing. Never "furthermore."
  • Superlatives → Replace with specific numbers. "Revolutionary" → "3x faster than the previous version."
  • Passive voice → Name the actor. "It was decided" → "We decided."
  • Jargon → Say what you actually mean. "Leverage synergies" → "work together."

Step 5: Re-score and Iterate

Run the updated text through again. Aim for under 20%. If you're still over 30%, you're probably still hedging — cut more.

Common AI Slop Patterns (And How to Fix Them)

The "In Today's [Adjective] [Noun]" Opening

"In today's fast-paced digital world, businesses must adapt to ever-changing consumer demands."
"Your customers don't care about 'digital transformation.' They care about whether your app loads in under 2 seconds."

The "It's Important to Note" Qualifier

"It's important to note that this approach can significantly enhance overall productivity."
"This approach doubled our team's output."

The "Leverage Synergies" Special

"By leveraging synergies across departments, organizations can unlock unprecedented value."
"When marketing and sales share the same CRM data, close rates go up 30%."

The "Dive Deep" Cliché

"Let's dive deep into the multifaceted landscape of modern content strategy."
"Here's exactly how we plan content for a $2M product launch."

The "Not Only... But Also" Construction

"This solution not only streamlines workflows but also empowers teams to achieve their full potential."
"It saves 4 hours per week. Your team can use that time however they want."

7 Quick Fixes to De-Slop Any Text

  1. Delete your first paragraph. AI almost always starts with a general statement. Start with the specific point instead.
  2. Replace "leverage" with the actual verb. Use, try, apply, build, combine — anything but leverage.
  3. Cut every "important to note" and "worth mentioning." If it's important, just say it.
  4. Add specific numbers. "Many" → "47%". "Significant" → "3.2x". "Various" → "three specific".
  5. Write like you talk. Read your text out loud. If you'd never say "furthermore" in conversation, don't write it.
  6. Use contractions. "It is" → "it's". "Do not" → "don't". Real humans contract.
  7. Add opinion. AI is neutral. You're not. Say "I think", "here's why that's wrong", or "the best approach is..." — commit to a position.
