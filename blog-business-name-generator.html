16 Naming Strategies That AI Uses (With Examples)

Professional naming agencies use a handful of proven strategies. AI applies all 16 simultaneously:

1. Compound Words
Two words merged: Facebook, YouTube, Pinterest
2. Portmanteau
Blend two words: Instagram (instant + telegram), Pinterest
3. Abstract / Evocative
No literal meaning, evokes feeling: Google, Yahoo, Zumba
4. Metaphor
Symbolic reference: Amazon (vast), Tesla (inventor), Mars
5. Descriptive
Says what it does: General Motors, Bank of America
6. Acronym
Letters form identity: IBM, BMW, KFC
7. Domain-Style
Web-native names: Dropbox, Spotify, Hulu
8. Action Verb
Verb-based: Zoom, Stripe, Square
9. Misspelling
Intentional typo: Lyft, Fiverr, Reddit
10. Foreign Word
Non-English origin: Häagen-Dazs, Volvo (Latin for "I roll")
11. Alliteration
Repeated sounds: Coca-Cola, Dunkin' Donuts
12. Prefix/Suffix
Add a modifier: Shopify, Notion, Grammarly
13. Number-Based
Numeric element: 7-Eleven, 3M, Fab
14. Founder Name
Person's name: Ford, Dell, Ben & Jerry's
15. Opposite / Paradox
Contradiction: Silent Disco, Jumbo Shrimp
16. Rhyming / Playful
Fun to say: HubSpot, FitBit, DogDog

The 7-Point Name Evaluation Checklist

Once AI generates 50+ names, narrow down with this checklist:

1.
Short (1–3 words) — Long names get abbreviated anyway. "International Business Machines" → IBM.
2.
Easy to spell — If people can't spell it, they can't find it. Avoid creative spellings unless iconic (Lyft works; Phytox doesn't).
3.
Easy to pronounce — Say it out loud. If you hesitate, your customers will too.
4.
Domain available — Check if the .com is available or affordable. Also check .ai, .io, .co alternatives.
5.
Not trademarked — Search USPTO.gov for existing trademarks. This saves $10K+ in legal headaches.
6.
Right emotional tone — A fintech startup named "FluffyCloud" sends the wrong signal. Match name vibe to industry.
7.
Global check — Make sure the name doesn't mean something offensive in another language (especially if you plan to go international).
