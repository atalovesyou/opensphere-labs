7 Clauses Every Freelance Contract Needs

Skip any of these and you're leaving money on the table — or working for free.

1

Scope of Work

Exactly what you'll deliver. Be specific — number of pages, revisions, formats, features. Vague scope = scope creep.

2

Payment Terms

Total amount, payment schedule (50/50, 30/40/30, milestone-based), due dates, accepted methods. Include late fee: 1.5%/month is standard.

3

Revision Limits

Number of included revision rounds (2 is standard). Additional revisions bill at $X/hr or $Y per round. Without this, you'll do 15 rounds for free.

4

IP Ownership Transfer

When does the client own the work? Standard: upon full payment. Until then, you retain ownership. This is your leverage.

5

Kill Fee / Termination

If the client cancels midway, they pay for work done + a kill fee (typically 25-50% of remaining value). Without this, a cancelled project = $0.

6

Late Payment Penalty

1.5% monthly interest on overdue invoices + pause work after 15 days late. Turns "I'll pay next month" into "here's the check."

7

Dispute Resolution

Mediation first, then binding arbitration. Specify jurisdiction (your city). Prevents clients from dragging you into court across the country.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are these freelance contract templates really free?

Yes. All 12 templates are free to download and customize. No signup, no credit card, no paywall. The ContractPilot tool lets you generate, customize, and export contracts as PDF for $0.

Which contract template should I use?

Match the template to your project type: Web Development for dev projects, Brand & Design for logos/branding, Content Writing for blog/copy work, Consulting for advisory services, and SOW (Statement of Work) for detailed project specs. If unsure, start with the General Service Agreement.

Do I need a lawyer to review my freelance contract?

For most freelance projects under $10K, a well-structured template with protective clauses is sufficient. For larger projects ($10K+), international clients, or complex IP terms, a lawyer review is worth the $300-500 investment.

What clauses should every freelance contract include?

Every freelance contract needs: scope of work, payment terms (amount + schedule), revision limits, IP ownership, kill fee / termination clause, late payment penalty, and dispute resolution. These 7 clauses protect you from scope creep, non-payment, and IP disputes.
