The Freelance Rate Formula

(Target Income + Expenses + Tax Buffer) ÷ Billable Hours = Your Rate
Then add 20-40% for scope creep, late payments, and project gaps

Most new freelancers make one critical mistake: they take their old salary and divide by 2,080 (40 hours × 52 weeks). That formula assumes you bill every hour of every week. Reality: you'll bill 25-30 hours per week and work 46-48 weeks per year. The rest is admin, marketing, sales, and gaps between projects.

Step-by-step calculation:

  1. Target take-home: $80,000/year (what you want in your pocket)
  2. Add tax buffer (30%): $80,000 × 1.3 = $104,000
  3. Add business expenses: $104,000 + $12,000 = $116,000
  4. Billable hours: 28 hrs/week × 48 weeks = 1,344 hours
  5. Base rate: $116,000 ÷ 1,344 = $86.31/hr
  6. Add scope creep buffer (25%): $86.31 × 1.25 = $107.89 → $110/hr

This is your minimum viable rate. Your actual rate should be 10-30% above this to leave room for negotiation.

3 Pricing Models (When to Use Each)

⏱️ Hourly

  • ✅ Fair for both sides
  • ✅ Easy to quote
  • ✅ Scope changes don't matter
  • ❌ Punishes efficiency
  • ❌ Income ceiling
  • ❌ Clients watch the clock

Best for: Research, consulting, open-ended dev, discovery phases

📦 Project-Based

  • ✅ Rewards speed/skill
  • ✅ Client knows cost upfront
  • ✅ Higher effective hourly
  • ❌ Scope creep risk
  • ❌ Hard to estimate initially
  • ❌ Underquoting hurts

Best for: Design, copywriting, landing pages, defined deliverables

🔄 Retainer

  • ✅ Predictable income
  • ✅ Deeper client relationship
  • ✅ Less sales overhead
  • ❌ Client may over-use
  • ❌ Hard to raise rates
  • ❌ Scope can blur

Best for: Ongoing work, advisory, maintenance, content production

The play: Use all three. Quote hourly for discovery, project-based for deliverables, and convert happy clients to retainers. That's the freelance pricing trinity.

9 Pricing Strategies That Win Projects

1. Anchor High, Negotiate to Target

Best for: New clients, one-off projects

Quote 20-30% above your target. If your target is $100/hr, quote $120-130. The client negotiates down, you land at your real rate, and the client feels like they got a deal. This is the single most effective pricing technique.

2. Tiered Packages (Good / Better / Best)

Best for: Service offerings, productized services

Offer 3 tiers at $1,500 / $3,000 / $5,000. Most clients pick the middle — which is your target. The low tier exists to make the middle look reasonable. The high tier captures premium buyers. Never offer just one price.

3. Value-Based Pricing

Best for: Measurable outcomes (revenue, leads, efficiency)

Price based on the value you deliver, not the hours you work. If your work generates $50K in revenue for the client, charging $5-10K is fair regardless of whether it takes you 10 hours or 100 hours. This is how you break the hourly ceiling.

4. The "Plus Expenses" Model

Best for: Agency work, large projects, travel required

Quote a project fee plus a separate "expenses" line for software, stock assets, travel, and third-party services. This protects your margin from cost overruns and makes the fee look cleaner.

5. Sprint Pricing (Time-Boxed)

Best for: MVP builds, audits, rapid turnaround

"I'll deliver X in 2 weeks for $Y." Time-boxed sprints create urgency for the client and protect you from scope creep — when the sprint ends, the engagement ends or renews. Popular in dev and design.

6. Phase-Based Pricing

Best for: Large projects with uncertain scope

Break the project into phases: Discovery ($2K) → Design ($5K) → Build ($8K) → Launch ($3K). Each phase is a separate commitment. Reduces risk for both sides. If Discovery reveals the project is a mess, you can walk or reprice.

7. Day Rate (Not Hourly)

Best for: On-site work, workshops, intensive sessions

Sell days, not hours. A $800/day rate sounds better than $100/hr — and it gives you flexibility within the day. Clients book you for the day, they get your full attention, and you don't get nickel-and-dimed on hours.

8. The Retainer Ladder

Best for: Converting project clients to ongoing

After delivering a successful project, pitch a retainer: "I can continue to be available for ad-hoc needs at $1,500/month (up to 10 hours) or $3,000/month (up to 25 hours)." Pre-sells your time at a slight discount for guaranteed income.

9. Performance-Based (Skin in the Game)

Best for: High-confidence projects, lead gen, conversion optimization

Lower your base fee + take a percentage of results. "I'll build your funnel for $3,000 base + 10% of revenue it generates in the first 90 days." Riskier, but the upside is uncapped. Only do this when you control the outcome.

Negotiation Scripts (Copy & Paste)

💬 When the client says "That's too expensive"
"I understand budget matters. Let me ask — what's the cost of not doing this project? If [problem] continues for another quarter, what does that cost you? My fee is an investment in solving that permanently. That said, I can scope this down to phase 1 only at [$X] — would that work?"
💬 When the client says "Can you do it for less?"
"I can adjust the scope to fit your budget. At [$lower amount], I can deliver [reduced scope]. For the full scope we discussed, [$original amount] is what it takes to do it right. Which would you prefer?"
💬 When raising your rate with existing clients
"Quick heads-up — my rate is adjusting to [$new rate] effective [30 days from now]. I wanted to give you advance notice so we can plan. I've loved working together and this ensures I can continue giving your projects my full attention. Happy to chat about it."
💬 When a client asks for free work "to see if we're a fit"
"I don't do unpaid trials, but I do offer a [1-hour strategy call / mini-audit] for [$X — usually 25% of your rate]. It gives you real value and lets us both evaluate the fit. Want to book one?"
