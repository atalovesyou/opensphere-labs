How to Choose the Right Contract Tool

You're a freelancer on a budget

→ ContractPilot (free) or ShouldISign.ai (free review). Start with ContractPilot for generation, use ShouldISign for a second opinion on client contracts. Both free.

You're a team that needs contract workflows

→ Juro ($1,250/mo) or Ironclad ($2,500/mo). These are enterprise tools — worth it if you're managing 100+ contracts/month.

You need business formation (LLC, trademark)

→ LegalZoom ($20/mo). They're good at this. Just don't use them for freelance contracts.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is ContractPilot really free?

Yes. The free tier includes 3 contracts/month with full AI generation, review, and negotiation scripts. No credit card required. No signup required. Just open the page and start.

Are the contracts legally binding?

ContractPilot generates contract templates based on standard freelance contract structures. They're a strong starting point, but we always recommend having a lawyer review important contracts. Think of it as a first draft that catches 90% of common issues — the lawyer handles the final 10%.

How does the contract review work?

Paste any client contract into ContractPilot's review engine. It scans for 40+ risky clause patterns including unlimited revisions, vague scope definitions, missing payment milestones, IP assignment traps, penalty clauses, and non-compete overreach. Each flagged clause comes with a pushback script in 3 tones.

What about e-signatures?

ContractPilot focuses on contract creation, review, and negotiation — not signing. For e-signatures, pair it with a free tool like DocuSign (3 free/month) or HelloSign (3 free/month). The PDF export from ContractPilot works with any signing platform.

Why not just use LegalZoom?

LegalZoom is excellent for business formation (LLCs, trademarks, DBAs). But for freelance contracts specifically, they offer static templates at $20+/month with no AI analysis, no risk detection, and no negotiation help. ContractPilot is purpose-built for freelancers at $0.
